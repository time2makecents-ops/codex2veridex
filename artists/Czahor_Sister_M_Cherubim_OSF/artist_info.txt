SISTER M. CHERUBIM CZAHOR, OSF
Working research file - corrected September 13, 2026

PURPOSE
This dossier supports research into a framed pastel portrait of a young boy signed by Sister M. Cherubim Czahor, OSF. It separates verified facts, physical observations, research leads, and unresolved questions. It is not an authentication or appraisal.

VERIFIED IDENTITY
Religious name: Sister M. Cherubim Czahor, OSF
Birth name: Josephine Rita Czahor
Born: March 17, 1916, Chicago, Illinois
Baptized: St. Adalbert's, Chicago
Died: February 28, 2008, Joliet, Illinois, age 91
Religious community: Sisters of St. Francis of Mary Immaculate (Joliet Franciscans)
Parents: Vincent Czahor and Marcella (Gorczyca) Czahor
Siblings named in Franciscan records: Frank Czahor, Stanley Czahor, Marie/Marcie Czahor Korus, and Sophie Czahor Wisocky

Important correction: The Chicago Artists' File index gives "1936-" after her name. She was born in 1916. August 12, 1936 was the date of her first religious profession.

RELIGIOUS LIFE
Postulancy: September 8, 1933
Novitiate: August 12, 1934; received the name Sister Cherubim
First profession: August 12, 1936
Final profession: August 12, 1939
Member of the Joliet Franciscan community for approximately 74 years

EDUCATION AND ARTISTIC TRAINING
- Bachelor degree, College of St. Francis, Joliet (now University of St. Francis)
- Master of Science in Education, Northern Illinois University
- Art studies at Siena Heights, Adrian, Michigan
- Art studies at Cooper School of Art, Cleveland, Ohio
- Art studies at the Art Institute of Chicago
- Nursing Assistant degree, Joliet Junior College

Qualification: An undated article excerpt says she had "a master's degree in art." The official obituary and Franciscan records identify the graduate degree as a Master of Science in Education. Do not describe it as an art degree unless an academic record or the original article confirms that detail.

TEACHING AND MINISTRY
Sister Cherubim taught grade school before becoming a long-serving art teacher in Illinois and Ohio. Her documented assignments included St. Francis Academy in Joliet, SS. Peter and Paul in Chicago, Marybrook Academy in Maumee, Holy Family in Joliet, Joliet Township High School, Joliet Artists League programs, and private lessons. She also taught history, French, English, ESL, religion, first aid, and Red Cross subjects; repaired religious statues; and provided nursing and pastoral assistance.

The Franciscan ministry chronology includes the impossible range "1990-1975" for her St. Francis Academy service. Based on the surrounding entries and her obituary, it almost certainly means approximately 1960-1975. The Franciscan archive should confirm this correction.

ARTISTIC CAREER
Sister Cherubim produced religious and secular paintings, ceramics, sculpture, and restoration work. She taught art for decades, gave private lessons, exhibited her work, and sold numerous paintings.

Her Franciscan biography states that she exhibited paintings, ceramics, or sculpture at:
- Toledo Museum of Art
- Joliet Artists League
- Czahor-Kock Show
- Religious Art Festival
- Aquinas Art Festival
- Pottery Expo in Munich, Germany

The biography states that she was invited more than once to exhibit paintings in France but declined because of shipping expense. It also states that she served on art boards and committees and was president of the Joliet Artists League for a period.

NEWSPAPER/ARTICLE LEAD - SOURCE NEEDED
An undated article excerpt describes Sister Mary Cherubim Czahor of Joliet as a longtime member of the Joliet Artists League. It says she taught art for approximately 60 years and quotes her explaining that the league gave her an outlet to show her work to people other than her students.

The phrase "29-year member" appears to refer to her Joliet Artists League membership, not her Franciscan religious service. Do not cite this passage externally until the newspaper title, author, date, and page are identified.

ART INSTITUTE OF CHICAGO LEAD
The Art Institute of Chicago Annual Report for 1995-1996 lists "Vincent and Marcella Czahor" under "Gifts in Memory" on printed page 85 (PDF page 88) in the local file aicarchives_13525.pdf.

Vincent and Marcella were Sister Cherubim's parents. This establishes a documented Czahor family memorial-gift connection to the Art Institute, but it does not establish who made the gift and does not prove that the Art Institute owns any of Sister Cherubim's artwork.

Contact: reference@artic.edu

FAMILY RESEARCH LEADS
Stephanie (Korus) Czahor was the wife of Sister Cherubim's brother Frank Czahor and therefore Sister Cherubim's sister-in-law. The local one-page obituary file "Czahor, Stephanie.pdf" documents that relationship.

Sophie Helen Czahor Wisocky did have children. Her obituary names three children: William Wisocky Jr., Rita Wisocky, and Sheila Lord. The earlier statement that Sophie had no children was false and came from mixing her up with a different person named Sophie Szydlowski.

Marie/Marcie Czahor Korus was Sister Cherubim's sister. She was not the painter of this portrait. The portrait is signed with Sister Cherubim's religious name and OSF affiliation.

Living relatives listed in public obituaries may know where Sister Cherubim's paintings went, but institutional archives should be contacted first. An archivist may be willing to forward an inquiry to an appropriate family representative without releasing private contact information.

Observed technique and condition:
- Dry, granular pigment is visible on textured paper.
- The portrait is protected behind glass.
- Reflections and surface dust complicate condition assessment.
- The pastel appears largely intact in the available photographs.
- The frame has a dark wood or ebonized appearance with gilt composition/gesso ornament.
- The frame shows chips, losses, abrasions, and wear.
- The artwork and frame should not be cleaned without conservation guidance.

Backing/framing observations:
- Dark backing board retained with old nails
- Remnants of brown paper dust covering
- Hanging wire and eye attachment visible
- No readable gallery, framer, exhibition, or ownership label found
- Several visible finger and hand impressions occur on the dark backing board

The impressions may have been deposited during original handling or framing and could potentially be associated with the artist if the assembly has remained undisturbed. That attribution is not proven: a framer, owner, or later handler could also have made them. Preserve them as part of the object's history. Do not wipe, clean, powder, spray, tape, or repeatedly touch the backing board.

CURRENT MARKET-RESEARCH STATUS
No dependable public auction record for Sister Cherubim's work has yet been located. Her documented training, exhibitions, teaching career, sales, and leadership establish her as a real regional artist, but they do not by themselves establish a current market price.

Any valuation should remain provisional until dimensions, date, condition, subject identity, provenance, comparable works, and confirmation from an archive or family collection are available.
